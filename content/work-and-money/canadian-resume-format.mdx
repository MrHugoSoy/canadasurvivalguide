Your resume worked in Mexico City, Bogotá, or Buenos Aires. It gets you nowhere in Toronto, Calgary, or Vancouver — and it's rarely about your experience. It's the format.

Canadian recruiters scan resumes for 6–8 seconds before deciding to read further or move on. If yours doesn't look like what they're used to seeing, it gets filtered before anyone actually evaluates whether you're qualified. Here's what's different, and how to fix it.

---

## No photo. Ever.

In most of Latin America, a professional photo on your CV is standard — sometimes expected. In Canada, it's the opposite: including one can get your resume flagged or quietly discarded by companies with strict human-rights and anti-discrimination hiring policies. HR teams are trained to avoid any information that could bias a hiring decision, and a photo is the first thing that goes.

Same rule applies to marital status, age, and a photo of your signature. None of it belongs on a Canadian resume.

## "CV" becomes "Resume" — and it's shorter

In LATAM, a CV can run 3–4 pages and includes your full academic history, every course, every certification. In Canada:

- **1 page** if you have under 10 years of experience
- **2 pages** maximum otherwise
- No academic transcripts, no list of every training course you've ever taken

Canadian employers want the highlights that are relevant to *this* job, not your complete professional biography.

## Achievements, not responsibilities

This is the single biggest gap. A LATAM-style resume often reads like a job description:

> "Responsible for managing the sales team and reporting to the regional director."

A Canadian resume reads like a results report:

> "Led a team of 8 sales reps, growing regional revenue 23% year-over-year and reducing turnover by 15%."

Every bullet point should answer: *what did you change, and by how much?* If you don't have exact numbers, estimate honestly ("approximately," "an estimated") rather than dropping the metric entirely. Recruiters trust specific numbers far more than vague responsibility statements — even estimated ones.

## Keywords matter more than you'd expect

Most mid-size and large Canadian companies run resumes through an ATS (Applicant Tracking System) before a human ever sees them. These systems scan for keywords pulled directly from the job posting. If the posting says "stakeholder management" and your resume says "coordinated with clients," the ATS may not connect the two — and your resume gets auto-filtered out.

**The fix:** pull the exact phrases from the job description and mirror them in your resume and summary, as long as they honestly describe your experience. Don't keyword-stuff — just don't paraphrase away the terms that matter.

## Format: clean, not creative

Colored sidebars, graphics, icons, and multi-column layouts are common in LATAM resume templates (especially ones downloaded from Canva). Most of them **break ATS parsing** — the software can't read text inside columns or graphic elements correctly, so your experience gets scrambled or dropped entirely before a human sees it.

Canadian-standard format:
- Single column
- Arial, Calibri, or Helvetica, 10–11pt
- Black text, white background
- Bold section headers, no color blocks
- Saved as a .docx or a text-based PDF (not a scanned image)

## Skip the objective statement, add a summary

"Objective: Seeking a challenging position where I can grow professionally" tells a recruiter nothing and is considered outdated in Canada. Replace it with a 2–3 line summary that states who you are professionally and what you bring:

> "Bilingual project manager with 6 years of experience delivering software implementations for financial services clients in Latin America. PMP-certified, fluent in English and Spanish, experienced leading distributed teams across time zones."

## References: "available upon request" is unnecessary

Canadian employers assume you'll provide references if asked — writing "references available upon request" wastes a line. Just omit it. Have 2–3 references ready separately, ideally people who can speak to your work in English.

## A quick before/after

| LATAM-style | Canadian-style |
|---|---|
| Photo included | No photo |
| 3 pages, full history | 1–2 pages, relevant highlights |
| "Responsible for X" | "Achieved X, measured by Y" |
| Colored template with icons | Clean single-column, no graphics |
| Objective statement | Professional summary |
| Every course and certificate listed | Only certifications relevant to the role |
